# Reassessment after the operating benchmark and current CI repair

The benchmark now has a useful execution boundary: a real GStreamer baseline/shared pair for each analytics resource, with one owner, bounded lifetime, authenticated guardian cleanup, complete cold reconciliation and independent raw reduction. CPU08 and GPU02 each completed both arms at source B a00aa57f7d9534f8e7920f14f70d6a75a23570ed. Their original CLI exits were zero, all 95 input hashes and Linux file identities stayed fixed, and the reserves and owned processes were released. The four-arm reducer independently reproduced every carried result. This establishes an operating, interpretable component benchmark.

My earlier approach coupled the first usable result to too many different questions: broad qualification, every backend, remote capacity, full publication authority and long campaigns. The practical architectural change is the separate component CLI and its narrow authority. It reuses the existing native, model, physical-input and domain validators. A private owner/root/authority-bound validation session avoids repeating the same full assessment at every internal helper boundary, while public validators remain independent and live input/image checks remain in place. A private Linux ext4 root makes those physical checks practical. The accepted runs support this design; they do not give a controlled storage or validation speedup estimate.

The new boundary also preserves meaning. Component results cannot promote themselves into qualification, accepted-policy, Q4 or full-publication authority. The original 32-arm, 1120-operation and 5600-operation campaigns remain recorded as unexecuted. The named full-consumer guards have source review, but direct negative regression coverage of every consumer is incomplete. That limitation must remain visible in conformance rather than disappearing behind two narrower component tests or a successful portable suite.

I revised my causal interpretation of CPU07. Its worker completed an inference response before frontend delivery failed with EPIPE. The old inference label and missing native failure bytes obscured that distinction. The underlying socket-death cause remains unknown; the later Docker exit-zero event does not explain it. The repair captures the first validated worker before teardown, distinguishes invocation from delivery and preserves the original failure channels and primary error. CPU08/GPU02 success supports the current operating route without changing the historical failed result.

The CI clock failure was a separate concrete defect. A real kernel audit timestamp could lie outside the old fine-clock join because it came from the coarse clock. The current producer samples CLOCK_REALTIME_COARSE around the original syscall interval; the consumer requires versioned, same-owner, same-boot and correctly ordered evidence while retaining the journal, operation and policy guards. A meaningful old-code failure and 51 focused regressions support the change. Original hosted D40 then genuinely loaded and unloaded its bounded profile and passed the full suite: 3001 discovered, 2992 selected, 2904 successful and 88 individually audited skips, with zero failures/errors/missing required successes and all six native targets built. Its exact audit value joined the inclusive coarse bins. The sole stale selected-image test from P/C was repaired by three literal operands bound to the already committed genuine receipt; every assertion remained.

The first local C suite stopped before builds/tests when an HTTP200 body read timed out. The new D checkout copies the eight already verified assets into exclusive files and leaves the stock verifier responsible for size, SHA256, SHA384 and physical identity. That unchanged verifier actually accepted all eight as reused. The failed C attempt remains failed. I also omitted required wrapper arguments on an initial D invocation; it stopped at the usage check before creating output or launching CI, and I preserved that mistake before the one correct original CI dispatch.

The scientific result remains largely negative. CPU baseline/shared completed 516/664 of 1080 admitted frames and met 100 ms for 0/0. GPU completed 1070/1075 and met it for 3/1. Completed-frame medians were 5727.5/3621 ms on CPU and 2127/2168 ms on GPU. Different completed subsets prevent interpreting the median difference as a matched-frame causal speedup. Drops stay in the ingress denominator, receive no invented latency, and no frames were censored.

Six logical streams replay two recordings, with NVDEC in both analytics-resource modes. Numerical parity supports the declared topology/load proxy, not application accuracy. One baseline-first pair per resource cannot separate order, thermal or shared-resource effects or support population confidence intervals. Partial C_obs is attributed elapsed stage time, including overlap; a lower shared value establishes no processor-work, energy or decoder-utilization saving. The original workload and 100 ms target stayed fixed. An operating benchmark is valuable precisely because it now exposes these failures honestly.

The remaining architectural risk is the size of the control and evidence layer. Its purpose is to validate one original run behind a usable command and preserve the first failure. More snapshots, wrappers or review receipts are justified only by a concrete uncertainty. I would now stop speculative native redesign and keep the public operating command stable while investigating performance through controlled, separately specified experiments.
